# Abstract

**Thermoelastic Analysis of Porous Graphene-Platelet-Reinforced Cylinders under Thermo-Mechanical Loading Using the Lord-Shulman Theory**

This thesis investigates the thermoelastic analysis of porous cylinders reinforced with graphene platelets under thermo-mechanical loading, using Lord-Shulman theory. The hollow cylinders studied consist of layers with properties that vary through the thickness, arising from changes in the graphene platelet volume fraction and the porosity percentage in each layer.

The temperature distribution, stresses, strains, and displacements in these hollow cylinders are examined. For this purpose, the Lord-Shulman equations of motion and energy conservation are used to derive the governing equations, boundary conditions, and compatibility conditions. The efficient differential quadrature method is then used to discretize the equations of motion, boundary conditions, and compatibility conditions in space, and the Newmark method is used in time.

The elastic properties of the layers are calculated using the Halpin-Tsai theory and the rule of mixtures. The open-cell theory is also used to account for property changes due to porosity.

Because the Lord-Shulman theory limits the propagation speed of the thermal wave, the accuracy of the temperature calculation increases, and the temperature history shows a "peak-trough" pattern caused by thermal wave reflection. Placing conductive graphene platelets at the hot inner surface together with insulating porosity at the cold outer surface acts synergistically and markedly reduces the outer-surface temperature. Under thermal shock loading, the classical Fourier theory underestimates the peak temperature by about 2.5-fold, which is a strong argument in favor of the generalized theory. Methodologically, the combination of differential quadrature and Newmark integration achieves the desired accuracy at low computational cost.

The results show that using the generalized Lord-Shulman theory is essential for accurately analyzing transient thermal shock, since the classical theory severely underestimates transient stresses. From a design perspective, aligning the graphene-platelet and porosity distribution patterns can provide the most favorable thermal behavior at the lowest stress cost, though pattern selection should be judged from both temperature and stress standpoints simultaneously. The layerwise differential quadrature method combined with Newmark integration provides an efficient, validated tool for this type of coupled thermoelastic analysis.

**Keywords:** thermoelastic analysis, porous cylinder, graphene platelets, thermo-mechanical loading, Lord-Shulman theory.
