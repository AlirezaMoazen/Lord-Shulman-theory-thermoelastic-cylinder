# Abstract

**Thermoelastic analysis of graphene platelets reinforced porous cylinders under thermo-mechanical load based on Lord-Shulman theory**

Alireza Oliasami

This thesis investigates the thermoelastic analysis of porous cylinders reinforced with graphene platelets under thermo-mechanical loading using the Lord-Shulman theory. By introducing a relaxation time, the Lord-Shulman theory makes the thermal wave speed finite and also couples the temperature and displacement fields. The cylindrical shells under study consist of layers whose properties differ through the thickness, owing to the variation of the graphene platelet volume fraction and the porosity percentage from layer to layer. The elastic properties of the layers are computed using the Halpin-Tsai theory and the rule of mixtures, and the property changes due to porosity are computed using the open-cell theory; the distribution of both factors through the thickness is considered in five different patterns. The equations of motion and the Lord-Shulman energy conservation equation, together with the boundary and layer-compatibility conditions, are used in coupled form, and they are discretized with the differential quadrature method in space and the Newmark method in time. The accuracy of the solution is confirmed against exact analytical solutions and published results, and its convergence with respect to the radial and axial points and the time step is demonstrated. The thermal and mechanical loading considered in this study is a sudden rise in the inner-surface temperature together with an internal pressure, both of which are axisymmetric. Because the thermal wave speed is finite, the temperature history exhibits a back-and-forth pattern with reflection of the thermal wave, and under impulsive thermal loads the Fourier theory predicts a lower maximum temperature than the Lord-Shulman theory; hence, under sudden thermal loads the Fourier theory leads to a non-conservative estimate of the temperature, and the wave behavior must be taken into account. With the total amount of graphene platelets held constant, concentrating the conductive platelets at the hot inner surface reduces both the maximum temperature and the inner-surface hoop stress relative to a uniform distribution; therefore, the way the graphene platelets are distributed through the thickness, and not only their amount, affects the thermal and mechanical response. Porosity also acts as a thermal barrier: concentrating the pores at the outer surface lowers the outer-surface temperature, but it changes the sign of the hoop stress at the inner surface. The arrangement of the graphene platelet and porosity patterns can produce a synergistic or a neutralizing behavior. For instance, concentrating the platelets at the inner surface (the conductor facing the heat source) together with concentrating the pores at the outer surface (the insulator facing the environment) gives a lower temperature than concentrating both the platelets and the pores at the inner surface. Consequently, the graphene platelet and porosity distributions cannot be chosen independently of each other, and since a distribution that is favorable in terms of temperature is not always favorable in terms of stress, the choice must be made simultaneously on the basis of an analysis of both the temperature and the stress fields. Increasing the weight fraction of graphene platelets increases the thermal penetration and decreases the displacement; however, the effect of the platelets on conductivity depends on their forming a conductive network, so the improvement in conductivity begins only beyond a certain threshold of platelet addition, and the geometry of the platelets is also important. The response of the cylindrical shells is governed mainly by the thermal load, while the internal pressure and the support conditions have a smaller effect; therefore, in preliminary design the thermal field can be given priority. The differential quadrature method reaches comparable accuracy with fewer points than the finite difference and finite element methods, and among the time integrators tested the Newmark method offers the better accuracy-to-cost ratio; this indicates that the combination of differential quadrature and Newmark is an efficient framework for the transient coupled analysis of multilayer cylindrical shells.

**Keywords:** Thermoelastic analysis; porous cylinders; graphene platelets; thermo-mechanical load; Lord-Shulman theory
